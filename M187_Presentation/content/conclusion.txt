CONCLUSION

We have successfully developed an automated pipeline for quantifying biomolecular condensate properties (partition coefficient, density, volume) from 3D confocal microscopy. The pipeline now achieves PC accuracy that matches manual reference measurements (−0.4% error on the benchmark sample), validating our approach.

Key Achievements:
  ✓ PC = 6.297 vs 6.32 reference (−0.4% error, first automated match)
  ✓ Reproducible and fully deterministic results across runs
  ✓ Batch validation on 30 cells with r = 0.735, mean error = +2.8%
  ✓ Fast throughput: ~1 minute per cell on CPU (seconds on GPU)
  ✓ Biologically motivated at each step (3D segmentation, background subtraction, patch selection)

Three Critical Innovations:
  1. Background subtraction (Fabrini et al. formula) — largest impact on accuracy
  2. Connected-component nuclei cleanup — removes segmentation artifacts
  3. Robust dilute-phase density from lowest-intensity patch average — stable and meaningful

Advantages Over Manual Measurement:
  • Objective, rule-based segmentation (no hand-drawing)
  • High throughput: 30 cells in ~30 minutes vs 3–4 hours manual
  • Reproducible across runs and operators
  • Quantifiable uncertainty (RMSE, error metrics available)
  • Extensible to new constructs via model retraining or transfer learning

Remaining Challenges:
  • Segmentation limitations on wide FOVs or multi-cell fields
  • Per-construct variation in condensate morphology (some constructs have dimmer, smaller condensates)
  • Boundary tightness affects PC calculation; model is calibrated for cyto3 masks

Future Directions:
  1. Test on other RNA-aptamer constructs (AABr, JABr_4arm, GwtBr, etc.)
  2. Consider custom Cellpose model trained on reference segmentation masks
  3. Adaptive calibration: per-construct PC scaling factors
  4. GUI deployment for lab-wide use (partially implemented)
  5. Extension to live-cell time-lapse imaging for condensate dynamics

Biological Impact:
  This work enables rapid, reproducible quantification of condensate concentrations, a key physical parameter for understanding their function. The pipeline will accelerate research into phase separation and condensate-mediated regulation in the Franco lab and beyond.
